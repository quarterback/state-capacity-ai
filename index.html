State Capacity AI

State Capacity AI works at the intersection of public institution building and frontier technology. We conduct applied research, develop implementable doctrine, and build reference artifacts to help high-stakes sectors—from federal agencies to local services—responsibly harness new tools.

State capacity is the ability of an institution to do what it says it will do—with its own people, its own judgment, and its own infrastructure. AI doesn't change that requirement. It raises the stakes.

Anti-Delegation

The default trajectory of AI adoption is delegation: hand the task to the model, trust the output, move on. This works for low-stakes, high-volume tasks. It fails catastrophically for anything that requires judgment, context, or accountability.

Anti-delegation is not anti-AI. It is the discipline of knowing what you cannot outsource—and building the organizational muscle to keep doing those things yourself, better, with AI as a tool rather than a replacement.

Our work is guided by a focus on sustainable integration, aiming to augment core institutional functions and strengthen operational capacity. The guides below are the substance of that work.

Guides

AI Disclosure Standards

When government uses algorithms to make decisions about people's lives, no disclosure requirement exists. These are lightweight standards for closing that gap—enablement statements, a decision-grade taxonomy, and practice standards agencies can implement under pressure.
Agent Interface Standards

Draft standards for how autonomous agents interact with public service systems. Covers policy logic as versioned interfaces, action surfaces, handoff protocols, audit trails, context delivery, and public value principles.
Judgment Routing

Infrastructure for delegated authority and decision engineering in agentic AI. A middleware pattern that evaluates agent actions against policy constraints and authority boundaries, producing auditable decision receipts.
Parachute Commons License

A license that deploys protection as it gets closer to the ground. Starts permissive, escalates protections when risks like commercial extraction, automated ingestion, or misrepresentation are detected.

Design Principles

These guides are informed by five principles of context engineering design—a framework for thinking about AI deployment in environments where failure has real consequences.

  • Context as Environment, Not Just Input — Where the system is deployed shapes what success means.
  • Disintermediation Analysis — When does AI mediation separate people from capabilities?
  • Context Legibility — Making AI reasoning visible for oversight, not just for users.
  • Escalation & Refusal Design — Systems that know their limits and act on them.
  • Capability Preservation — AI should enhance human skill, not replace it.
