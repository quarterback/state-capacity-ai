Method III

The Shield

Open source without protection is a donation to the extraction economy. The Parachute Commons License is not a gate—it is a parachute. It starts permissive, but opens up with stronger protections when certain risks are detected.

The Problem of Delegation

Organizations share knowledge—frameworks, methods, institutional learning—under standard open-source licenses designed for code, not for organizational capacity. These licenses protect the right to copy and modify, but they do nothing to prevent extraction: the practice of taking knowledge, embedding it in proprietary systems, and selling it back to the people who created it.

In the AI era, this extraction is automated. Training data pipelines ingest open-source repositories. Model providers build commercial products on community knowledge. The original contributors receive nothing—not credit, not revenue, not even continued access to the derivatives built from their work.

The problem is deeper than piracy. It is the erosion of authorship in hyper-replicable environments and the collapse of clear attribution in knowledge systems. Standard open-source licenses were written for a world where "free as in speech" was sufficient. In a world of AI-powered extraction, freedom without protection is a subsidy to the largest players.

The Method of Capacity

The Parachute Commons License (PCL) 1.0 introduces expanding protection—a licensing model where protections escalate as risk increases. It is a social compact and community standard first, and a legal framework second.

Phase 1 — Permissive Foundation

The default state. The work is free to read, share, remix, and distribute. Attribution is required. This is the open commons as it should work—knowledge available to the people it was designed to help.

Phase 2 — Conditional Restrictions

Triggered automatically when specific risk conditions are detected:

  • Commercial use without consent
  • Large-scale automated ingestion (scraping, training data pipelines)
  • Use by organizations with 50+ employees
  • Bulk extraction or systematic reproduction

Under Phase 2, commercial use requires express written permission and revenue sharing. Attribution must explicitly state "Protected under Parachute Commons License."

Phase 3 — Full Protection

Activated for severe misuse:

  • Harmful impersonation or misrepresentation of authorship
  • Willful attribution violations
  • Use in surveillance systems
  • Deployment that causes demonstrable public harm

Under Phase 3, all use rights are suspended without written consent. This is the parachute deploying—protection that scales to match the threat.

Enforcement model

The PCL relies on shared norms and community pressure rather than purely legal mechanisms. Users can expand their permissions through written consent, perpetual attribution, fair compensation, or by contributing improvements back to the commons. The goal is not litigation—it is a credible framework for saying "this knowledge has terms."

Why this matters for state capacity

Public-sector organizations and community groups produce enormous amounts of valuable knowledge: policy frameworks, design patterns, service blueprints, operational playbooks. Under traditional licensing, this knowledge is free for anyone—including the vendors who will package it into products and sell it back as "AI-powered solutions." The Shield ensures that knowledge produced for the public good stays in the public good. Sharing builds commons; extraction depletes them.
