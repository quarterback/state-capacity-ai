Method IV

The Shield

Open source without protection is a donation to the extraction economy. The Parachute Commons License ensures that the more your work is used, the more protection it gains—not less.

The Problem of Delegation

Organizations share knowledge—frameworks, methods, institutional learning—under standard open-source licenses that were designed for code, not for organizational capacity. These licenses protect the right to copy and modify, but they do nothing to prevent extraction: the practice of taking knowledge, embedding it in proprietary systems, and selling it back to the people who created it.

In the AI era, this extraction is automated. Training data pipelines ingest open-source repositories. Model providers build commercial products on community knowledge. The original contributors receive nothing—not credit, not revenue, not even continued access to the derivatives built from their work.

Standard open-source licenses enable this by design. They were written for a world where "free as in speech" was sufficient. In a world of AI-powered extraction, freedom without protection is just a subsidy to the largest players.

The Method of Capacity

The Parachute Commons License introduces expanding protection—a licensing model where protections increase as usage scales. Small teams and individuals use the work freely. As adoption grows, so do the obligations.

How expanding protection works

  • Individual and small-team use: Fully open. No restrictions beyond attribution. The knowledge is free for the people it was designed to help.
  • Organizational adoption: Requires contribution-back. If you embed this work in your internal systems, improvements and adaptations must be shared with the commons.
  • Commercial extraction: Requires explicit licensing. If you use this work to build a commercial product—especially one that competes with the original contributors—you need a commercial agreement.
  • AI training use: Restricted. The work may not be used to train models without explicit permission, ensuring that organizational knowledge isn't silently absorbed into proprietary systems.

Why this matters for state capacity

Public-sector organizations and community groups produce enormous amounts of valuable knowledge: policy frameworks, design patterns, service blueprints, operational playbooks. Under traditional licensing, this knowledge is free for anyone—including the vendors who will package it into products and sell it back as "AI-powered solutions."

The Shield ensures that knowledge produced for the public good stays in the public good. It doesn't prevent sharing; it prevents extraction. The distinction is critical: sharing builds commons, extraction depletes them.
