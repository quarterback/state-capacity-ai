Method I

The Inventory

You cannot protect capacity you haven't mapped. Before deploying any AI agent, you must first understand what your people already know, what they already do, and what they will stop doing once the machine arrives.

The Problem of Delegation

Organizations adopt AI tools without first understanding their existing cognitive infrastructure. They automate processes before documenting what those processes actually require—the tacit knowledge, the judgment calls, the informal networks that make things work.

The result is predictable: institutional knowledge gets encoded into a system nobody understands, experienced staff lose the context that made them effective, and when the AI fails, there is no fallback because the original capability was never recorded.

This is not a technology problem. It is an inventory problem. You delegated something you hadn't catalogued, and now you can't get it back.

The Method of Capacity

The Inventory is a structured assessment of existing cognitive dependencies—a pre-deployment audit that maps the skills, knowledge, relationships, and judgment currently embedded in your organization.

What the assessment covers

  • Tacit knowledge mapping: What do experienced staff know that isn't written down? What decisions require context that lives in people's heads?
  • Dependency analysis: Which processes depend on human judgment that cannot be reliably automated? Where are the hidden handoffs?
  • Degradation modeling: If this capability is automated, what happens to the humans who currently perform it? What skills atrophy? What knowledge is lost?
  • Fallback planning: When the AI system fails—and it will—can your organization revert to manual operation? How quickly? At what cost?

The enablement framework

AI enablement is not AI adoption. Enablement means your organization gains capability. Adoption means your organization consumes a product. The inventory draws the line between the two by identifying where AI can genuinely augment human capacity and where it merely displaces it.

Specifically, the framework asks three questions before any deployment:

  1. What exists? Document the current state of human capability in the target area.
  2. What changes? Model the specific shifts in skill, knowledge, and workflow that automation will produce.
  3. What remains? Ensure that critical capabilities are preserved, not just transferred to a system.
