About This Initiative

Overview

The Agent-Mediated Public Services Standards initiative establishes baseline requirements for AI agents interacting with government services on behalf of citizens. Our goal is to ensure consistent, accessible, secure, and trustworthy experiences across all public sector digital interactions.

Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To create comprehensive, practical standards that enable government agencies to deliver agent-mediated services that are:

  • Accessible to all citizens regardless of ability, technical literacy, or circumstances
  • Secure in protecting sensitive personal and government data
  • Transparent about capabilities, limitations, and decision-making processes
  • Reliable in consistent performance and availability
  • Interoperable across different systems, agencies, and jurisdictions

Our Vision

We envision a future where AI agents seamlessly facilitate citizen access to government services, reducing barriers and improving outcomes for all. Through standardization, we can:

  • Eliminate friction in accessing essential services
  • Reduce costs through shared approaches and technologies
  • Build public trust through consistent, high-quality experiences
  • Enable rapid innovation while maintaining security and compliance
  • Foster collaboration across government levels and jurisdictions

Background & Context

The Agent Revolution in Public Services

AI agents are transforming how citizens interact with government. From scheduling appointments to filing taxes, from applying for benefits to accessing health services, agents are becoming the primary interface for many government interactions.

This transformation brings enormous opportunities but also significant challenges:

  • Equity concerns: Not all citizens have equal access or ability to use agent technologies
  • Security risks: Agents handle sensitive personal and government information
  • Trust issues: Citizens need confidence that agents work in their best interests
  • Fragmentation: Each agency developing custom solutions leads to inconsistent experiences
  • Integration complexity: Legacy systems weren't designed for agent interactions

Why Standards Are Essential

Without common standards, we risk creating a fragmented landscape where:

  • Citizens face different experiences across similar services
  • Vulnerable populations are excluded or underserved
  • Security vulnerabilities emerge from inconsistent implementations
  • Costs multiply through duplicated efforts
  • Integration becomes prohibitively complex

These standards provide a shared foundation that enables innovation while ensuring quality and consistency.

Guiding Principles

1. People-Centered Design

All standards prioritize the needs and experiences of citizens, particularly those who are most vulnerable or underserved. Technology serves people, not the other way around.

2. Universal Accessibility

Services must be accessible to everyone, regardless of disability, age, technical literacy, language, or socioeconomic status. Accessibility is not optional.

3. Privacy by Design

Privacy and security must be built into systems from the ground up, not added as afterthoughts. Citizens' data deserves maximum protection.

4. Radical Transparency

Citizens have a right to understand how agents work, what data they use, and how decisions are made. Transparency builds trust.

5. Open Standards

Standards should be openly developed, freely available, and vendor-neutral to ensure broad adoption and prevent lock-in.

6. Continuous Improvement

Standards must evolve with technology and feedback from real-world implementation. Flexibility and adaptation are essential.

Scope & Coverage

These standards apply to agent-mediated interactions across all public sector domains:

Service Areas

  • Health Services: Appointment scheduling, prescription management, health records access
  • Social Services: Benefits applications, eligibility checks, case management
  • Administrative Services: License renewals, permits, registrations
  • Tax Services: Filing, payments, refunds, queries
  • Legal Services: Court filings, legal aid access, document requests
  • Emergency Services: Non-emergency inquiries, service requests, status updates
  • Education Services: Enrollment, financial aid, records access

Interaction Types

  • Information retrieval and guidance
  • Form completion and submission
  • Appointment scheduling and management
  • Status checks and updates
  • Payment processing
  • Document upload and verification
  • Multi-step transaction management

Standards Development Process

Collaborative Approach

These standards are developed through an open, collaborative process involving:

  • Government agencies at federal, state, and local levels
  • Technology providers building agent systems and platforms
  • Accessibility experts ensuring inclusive design
  • Security professionals addressing privacy and protection
  • Policy makers aligning with regulatory requirements
  • Civil society organizations representing citizen interests
  • Academic researchers contributing evidence and insights

Iterative Refinement

Standards evolve through continuous cycles of:

  1. Research: Gathering evidence, best practices, and use cases
  2. Drafting: Creating initial standard proposals
  3. Review: Soliciting feedback from stakeholders
  4. Testing: Piloting standards in real implementations
  5. Revision: Incorporating lessons learned
  6. Publication: Releasing updated versions

Adoption & Implementation

Flexible Implementation

We recognize that government agencies vary widely in size, resources, and technical capability. These standards provide:

  • Tiered requirements: Essential (must have), recommended (should have), and optional (nice to have)
  • Phased adoption: Pathways for gradual implementation
  • Alternative approaches: Multiple ways to meet requirements
  • Implementation guidance: Practical advice for different contexts

Support Resources

To facilitate adoption, we provide:

  • Detailed implementation guides
  • Code examples and templates
  • Assessment tools and checklists
  • Training materials
  • Community support forums
  • Consulting and technical assistance

Governance & Maintenance

Open Governance Model

These standards are maintained as an open-source project with transparent governance:

  • Public repository: All standards documentation is openly accessible
  • Issue tracking: Anyone can propose changes or report issues
  • Review process: Changes undergo community review before adoption
  • Version control: Clear tracking of changes and rationale
  • Regular updates: Scheduled review and update cycles

Stewardship

The standards are stewarded by a diverse committee representing all stakeholder groups, ensuring balanced perspectives and broad legitimacy.
