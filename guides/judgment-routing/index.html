Judgment Routing

Infrastructure for delegated authority, judgment routing, and decision engineering in agentic AI systems.

The Problem

We are building AI systems that make consequential decisions about people's lives, and we have no shared infrastructure for making those decisions legible, bounded, or accountable.

Organizations are deploying AI agents to handle everything from benefits enrollment to procurement approvals. These systems work well until they encounter an edge case, an ambiguous policy interpretation, or a decision that exceeds their granted authority. When that happens, most systems either fail silently, hallucinate compliance, or execute anyway and hope nobody notices.

This creates two problems:

The 90/10 Problem

Ninety percent of decisions are routine and agents can handle them fine. The other ten percent carry outsized institutional risk. Without explicit routing logic, you cannot separate the two. The routine decisions and the dangerous ones flow through the same undifferentiated pipeline, with no mechanism to distinguish a standard refund from one that triggers regulatory exposure.

The Authority Problem

When a paper form moved through Jan to Bill to Steve, you knew who held signing authority at each step. The chain was visible. The routing was legible. When an AI processes a request, that chain disappears entirely. You are left trying to reconstruct decisions months later with no trail, no record of who authorized what, and no way to determine whether the action fell within any defined authority boundary.

What Judgment Routing Is

Judgment Routing is a stateful middleware pattern that sits between an agent's proposal and the execution of that action.

The mechanism works in three stages:

  1. The agent performs analysis and recommends an action.
  2. The judgment layer evaluates that recommendation against explicit policy constraints and authority boundaries.
  3. Based on the evaluation, the action either executes immediately, gets routed for verification, or escalates to a human with signing authority.

Every decision produces a receipt that explains what happened and why.

Core Concepts

Decision Engineering

Decision Engineering is the practice of making authority chains, policy constraints, and escalation rules explicit rather than implicit. It is the modern equivalent of visible paper-based authority. When Jan reviewed forms and passed edge cases to Steve, that was decision engineering—it was visible and traceable. We need the same clarity when AI systems process requests, only now it must be documented in machine-readable form.

Judgment Router

A Judgment Router is infrastructure that evaluates proposed actions against your institutional rules. It determines whether an action falls within approved parameters, requires additional verification, or needs human review.

Decision Receipts

Decision Receipts are structured records that link every action back to the authority that permitted it. They preserve institutional memory and create audit trails that can be inspected by oversight bodies, auditors, and affected parties.

The Four Signals

The router evaluates proposed actions across four signals:

Uncertainty

Measures data ambiguity or conflicting requirements. When the inputs to a decision are incomplete, contradictory, or drawn from unreliable sources, uncertainty is elevated. High uncertainty means the agent cannot confidently determine the correct action and the decision should not proceed without additional verification.

Stakes

Measures fiscal impact, downstream risk, or stakeholder count. A decision affecting one low-value transaction is fundamentally different from one affecting thousands of beneficiaries or triggering regulatory obligations. Stakes scoring ensures that the magnitude of potential consequences is explicitly weighted in the routing decision.

Authority

Compares required sign-off level against current delegation. Every action has an implicit authority requirement. Issuing a small refund may fall within an agent's delegated scope; modifying a contract never does. The authority signal checks whether the agent's current envelope grants sufficient authority for the proposed action, or whether escalation is required.

Novelty

Distinguishes familiar patterns from first-of-their-kind scenarios. An agent processing its thousandth standard refund operates with high confidence from precedent. An agent encountering a request type it has never seen before is operating without institutional memory. Novelty scoring ensures that unprecedented situations receive proportionally more scrutiny.

The Four Routing Paths

Based on the four signals, actions route through one of four paths:

Fast Path

For low-risk decisions that execute immediately. All four signals score below threshold. The action falls within delegated authority, involves familiar patterns, carries limited stakes, and relies on unambiguous data. The agent proceeds and a decision receipt is generated automatically.

Slow Path

For ambiguous cases requiring verification. One or more signals are elevated but none breach hard limits. The action may involve slightly unfamiliar patterns, moderate stakes, or minor data gaps. Execution pauses while the system performs additional validation, cross-references policy documents, or requests supplementary data before proceeding.

Human Escalation

For high-stakes decisions or authority gaps. The proposed action exceeds the agent's delegated authority, involves stakes above the defined threshold, or triggers a mandatory escalation rule. The system generates a structured decision package—a briefing optimized for fast, informed human judgment—and routes it to the appropriate authority.

Specialist Referral

For domain mismatches requiring expert review. The proposed action falls outside the agent's domain competence. A customer service agent encountering a legal question, a procurement agent facing a technical specification it cannot evaluate, or any scenario where the required expertise does not match the agent's capabilities. The system routes to a qualified specialist rather than allowing an unqualified agent to proceed.

Three Core Artifacts

A judgment router operates on three artifacts that form a chain from human intent to machine execution to auditable record.

1. Strategic Context Document

This is what a manager or executive actually writes. Natural language with explicit structure. It defines priorities, constraints, and authority levels in a format that is both human-readable and machine-parseable.

strategic_context:
  id: "q1-2026-customer-retention"

  active_period:
    start: "2026-01-01"
    end: "2026-03-31"

  authority_grantor:
    name: "Sarah Chen"
    role: "Head of Customer Success"

  priorities:
    customer_retention:
      weight: 0.9
      rationale: "Q1 target is 95% retention rate"

    cost_efficiency:
      weight: 0.4
      rationale: "Budget flexibility for high-value accounts"

    response_speed:
      weight: 0.8
      rationale: "Four-hour resolution target for P1 issues"

  authority_boundaries:
    financial:
      - action: "issue_refund"
        limit: 2000
        currency: "USD"
        escalation_threshold: 5000

      - action: "apply_discount"
        limit: 30
        unit: "percent"

    communication:
      - action: "send_customer_email"
        limit: 100
        unit: "recipients"
        escalation_threshold: 500

    legal:
      - trigger: "contract_modification"
        require_approval: true

      - trigger: "liability_language"
        require_approval: true

  escalation_contacts:
    - authority_level: "manager"
      name: "Sarah Chen"
      threshold: "exceeds financial limits"

    - authority_level: "legal"
      name: "Legal Team"
      threshold: "contract or liability issues"

The strategic context document encodes institutional intent. Priorities carry explicit weights so agents can resolve tradeoffs without guessing. Authority boundaries define hard limits. Escalation contacts ensure that when limits are exceeded, the system knows exactly who to ask.

2. Authority Envelope

The machine-readable container that agents carry during execution. Generated from the strategic context and scoped to specific tasks. It is the agent's credential—proof of what it is authorized to do, for how long, and under what constraints.

    "expires_at": "2026-01-14T18:23:00Z",

    "source_context": "q1-2026-customer-retention",
    "task_scope": "customer_issue_resolution",

    "granted_authorities": [
      {
        "action": "issue_refund",
        "max_amount": 2000,
        "requires_receipt": true
      },
      {
        "action": "apply_discount",
        "max_percentage": 30,
        "requires_receipt": true
      },
      {
        "action": "send_customer_email",
        "max_recipients": 100,
        "requires_receipt": true
      }
    ],

    "active_priorities": {
      "customer_retention": 0.9,
      "cost_efficiency": 0.4,
      "response_speed": 0.8
    },

    "mandatory_escalations": [
      "contract_modification",
      "liability_language",
      "amount > 5000",
      "recipients > 500"
    ]
  }
}

Authority envelopes are time-scoped and task-scoped. They expire. They carry only the authorities relevant to the current task. They list mandatory escalation triggers so the agent cannot accidentally bypass hard constraints. Every action the agent takes is checked against this envelope before execution.

3. Decision Receipt

Generated whenever authority is exercised. Designed for human review and audit. The receipt links the action back through the authority chain: what was proposed, how it was evaluated, whether it aligned with active priorities, and who granted the authority that permitted it.

Decision receipts close the accountability loop. Months later, an auditor can trace any action back to the strategic context that authorized it, the priority weights that influenced the evaluation, and the human who granted the authority in the first place. This is the paper trail that disappears when AI systems operate without judgment routing.

Evaluation Logic

The judgment router's evaluation follows a strict order of operations. Hard constraints are checked first. Authority boundaries are checked second. Priority alignment is calculated last. This ensures that no amount of strategic alignment can override a hard constraint or an authority gap.

def evaluate_action(envelope, proposed_action):
    for boundary in envelope.authority_boundaries:
        if violates_hard_constraint(proposed_action, boundary):
            return BLOCKED

    if exceeds_authority(proposed_action, envelope.granted_authorities):
        return ESCALATE

    alignment_score = calculate_alignment(
        proposed_action,
        envelope.active_priorities
    )

    return APPROVED_WITH_RECEIPT

The evaluation is deliberately simple. BLOCKED means a hard constraint was violated—the action cannot proceed regardless of context. ESCALATE means the action may be legitimate but exceeds the agent's current authority—a human with sufficient authority must decide. APPROVED_WITH_RECEIPT means the action falls within all boundaries and a decision receipt is generated for the audit trail.

The Request Flow

When an agent needs to take action, the request moves through three stages:

1. Preflight Check

The agent submits its proposed action along with its authority envelope to the judgment router. The envelope proves what the agent is authorized to do. The proposal describes what it wants to do. The router compares the two.

2. Evaluation

The router checks the proposed action against authority boundaries and priority weights. Hard constraints are evaluated first. Authority scope is evaluated second. Priority alignment is calculated third. The four signals—uncertainty, stakes, authority, novelty—are scored and used to determine the routing path.

3. Resolution

One of three outcomes is returned:

  • APPROVED — Action proceeds and a decision receipt is generated. The receipt captures the full evaluation context for audit purposes.
  • ESCALATE — Human decision required. The system generates a structured decision package optimized for fast, informed human judgment and routes it to the appropriate authority contact.
  • BLOCKED — Hard constraint violated. The action cannot proceed. The system records the attempted action and the specific constraint that prevented it.

What This Enables

Organizations implementing judgment routing gain:

  • Delegatable authority with explicit bounds. Managers define what agents can do, for how long, and under what constraints. Authority is granted, scoped, and revocable—not assumed.
  • Explainable, auditable decisions. Every action links back through a chain of receipts, envelopes, and strategic context documents. The reasoning is preserved, not lost.
  • Instant propagation of strategic priorities. When organizational priorities shift, updating the strategic context document immediately changes how every agent evaluates tradeoffs. No retraining required.
  • Human oversight focused on exceptions. Instead of reviewing every action or reviewing none, humans review only the decisions that exceed defined authority boundaries. The 90% that is routine flows through automatically. The 10% that matters gets human attention.
  • Trustable autonomy at scale. The mechanism stays consistent across domains. Start with one workflow—refund approvals, procurement, content moderation—and expand. Only the schemas evolve; the routing infrastructure remains the same.

Implementation

Start with one workflow and expand. The mechanism stays consistent; only the schemas evolve. Refund approvals, procurement decisions, benefits eligibility, content moderation—each domain defines its own strategic context documents and authority boundaries, but the routing infrastructure, evaluation logic, and receipt format remain the same across all of them.
